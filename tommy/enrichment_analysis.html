Enrichment analysis¶

During classification, we used many subgroups of genes to classify the mutations. There were some performing better, some performing worse. We would like to understand whether these genes change significantly between mutated and non mutated cells. To do this, we will perform enrichment analysis and the very famous method GSEA[1].

We will test the following group of genes obtained during the respective classification of CCLE and TGCA:

  1. Target genes of TP53;
  2. 100 most relevant genes in classification chosen from TP53 targets;
  3. 100 most relevant genes in classification chosen from the whole gene corpus;

NOTE: The 100 most relevant genes were chosen based on Spearman correlation coefficient [2] between expression and binary mutation type.

GSEA¶

Ingredients:

  1. Expression of samples belonging to two classes
  2. Gene set S
  3. Ranking method between expression and class distinction r

Intermediate Step:
We compute the correlation r between each gene array, expression of the gene across samples, and the mutation array. Then, we rank the gene based on r to have a ranked list L.

Goal:
Understand if the gene set S is randomly distributed around L or it is found at the top or at the bottom.

Methods:
GSEA computes an enrichment score ES which reflect the degree at which the set S is overrepresented at the extremes (top or bottom). In order to compute that we will use a Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistics, which measure maximum discrepancy between two cumulative distributions functions. In more simple words, we keep an array of the length of the whole gene corpus, which will keep a cumulative sum. It will be initiated at all zeros. Then, we iterate from the most correlated gene to the least, two possible cases can occur:

  1. The gene g is the gene set S, then: $$ RES[k] = RES[k-1] + \frac{|r(g)|^p}{|S|} $$
  2. The gene g is not in the gene set S, then: $$ RES[k] = RES[k-1] - \frac{1}{N -|S|} $$ Where, N is the number of genes and |S| is the cardinality of the gene set and p tells us how much we want to weight the correlation coefficient. Our ES will be the max of the cumulative sum, so the maximum deviation, the biggest period in which my function increased.

Discussion On one hand, if the set S is well concentrated on one part of the ranked list, we will keep summing and get an higher value. On the other hand, if the set is randomly distributed, we keep summing and subtracting without reaching a substantial value.

def standard(df):
    """
    Standardize value.

    Args:
        df (pandas.dataframe): dataframe.

    Returns:
        pandas.dataframe: Data after standardization.

    """
    dt = df.copy()
    #dt = dt.astype("float64")
    # function for data standardization
    gene_names = dt.columns
    cell_names = dt.index

    scaler = StandardScaler(with_mean=False)
    scaler.fit(dt)
    dt = scaler.transform(dt)

    dt = pd.DataFrame(dt)
    dt.columns = gene_names
    dt.index = cell_names

    return(dt)
